# Reference

Editorial style for the article. Distilled from toss.tech's editorial practice; directives only — the judgment stays with the writer. Article language follows the designated reader; 해요체 applies only when writing Korean.

## The axis

- **Question precedes concept.** Every concept enters only as the answer to a question the narrative has already created; the reader always knows why the next paragraph exists. Order by when the reader needs something, never by taxonomy.
- **Honesty guards trust.** Real failures, concrete numbers, admitted tradeoffs — the friendly tone stays credible only under this weight.
- **Relationships live in visuals.** Prose carries flow; the moment a structure or relation appears, a diagram takes the load.

## Shape

Open on a lived problem scene — a moment the reader has been in — and make the destination visible early. Narrow the surface symptom to the real problem, show why the obvious approach falls short, and let that demand summon each concept in turn. Close on results in real numbers and an honestly admitted tradeoff, compressed to a sentence or two. This is the default arc — let the material reshape it.

## Directives

- Headings are the reader's next question or the story's next beat; the heading list alone reads as the narrative.
- Mentor tone — explaining to the colleague at the next desk: conversational, exact, addressing the reader directly, with an occasional question mid-prose.
- Define a term the moment it first appears, in one concrete sentence; add an everyday analogy only when the concept stays abstract without one.
- Name the relationships between concepts explicitly in prose, and place a visual where a relation is first drawn, where structure changes, and where a result is claimed — every few paragraphs, as evidence, never decoration.
- Sandwich code: why it is needed → the code → what it bought, in numbers or plain words.
- Concrete over abstract, numbers over adjectives, limits over polish; short paragraphs, one new concept at a time.
